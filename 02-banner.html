Feature 02

Home page banner

The ask: reduce the height so it is clearer what Frontier does above the fold.

The current banner on the home page fills close to the full viewport with a rotating image slide and an overlay panel, so on a standard laptop almost nothing else is visible until you scroll. All three options below are drawn at roughly true height, with a marker showing where a 1440 × 800 laptop would cut off.

Idea 1

Split banner — proposition left, featured story right

The positioning line stops competing with the photography. At around 320px the banner, a full content section and the top of the next one all sit above the fold. The rotating featured stories survive, but as the smaller half of the layout.

  • 1What Frontier does is now text, not an image caption. The proposition and a plain-English second line sit in the first screen, with two routes onward — one for what you do, one for who does it.
  • 2The carousel is kept, but demoted. Three featured stories still rotate, in a small overlay card rather than a full-height slide. That preserves the promotion slot the team relies on without spending the whole viewport on it.

Idea 2

Statement band — no image at all

The most aggressive reduction. A short typographic band states the proposition and hands straight over to content. Everything the banner carousel used to do moves into the featured items module directly beneath it.

  • 3Three featured stories, fully visible, no rotation. Nothing is hidden behind a carousel the visitor has to wait for — a common complaint about slide-based banners, and it removes the promotion bottleneck of one slot at a time.
  • The sector strip answers “what do they do?” concretely and immediately, and gives the sector pages a strong entry point from the home page.

Idea 3

Image band with overlay card

Closest to the LCP reference

Keeps a strong image band, but caps it at a fixed height rather than the full viewport, with the story sitting in a floating card. A slim proposition bar directly beneath means the image is never the only thing on the first screen.
