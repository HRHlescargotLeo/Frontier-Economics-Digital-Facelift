Feature 03

People directory — landing page

The ask: a new template for the people landing page.

The current page is an alphabetical grid of 423 people across 34 pages, with a search box and a filter panel that opens as a full-width overlay. There is no result count, no indication of which filters are active once the panel closes, and the cards carry only a name and job title — so a visitor looking for the right expert has very little to go on.

Idea 1

Persistent filter rail with descriptive cards

Closest to the LCP reference

Filters stay visible down the left while results update on the right, so the visitor always knows what they are looking at. Each card carries a single line describing how that person helps clients — the change that does most to make the directory useful.

  • 1Result count and active filter chips. Tick some boxes on the left — the chips and the count update. The current overlay panel hides this state as soon as it closes, which is the main reason the filters feel unreliable.
  • 2One line per person, written in the first person. This is the LCP device Frontier liked, and it turns a staff list into something a prospective client can actually navigate. It does need a new short field on the person record, and 423 people to fill it — worth discussing whether it is mandatory, or shown only where written.
  • “Show more” replaces 34 pages of pagination. The current alphabetical paging means anyone whose surname begins after B is effectively unreachable by browsing.
Idea 2

Horizontal filters, A–Z jump and a view toggle

Closest to the NERA reference

Keeps the full-width grid Frontier has today but makes it navigable: dropdown filters across the top, an alphabet strip for people who know the name they want, and a list view for scanning a lot of people quickly.

  • 3The A–Z strip does two jobs. It gives a fast route to a known name, and greying out empty letters means the visitor never lands on a dead result set.
  • 4Try the grid / list toggle. List view fits roughly four times as many people on screen with office and expertise in aligned columns — much better for someone scanning a whole practice. It also loads far fewer photographs.
  • Filters as dropdowns keep the page width for results, but they hide the options until clicked. If Frontier expects most visitors to filter rather than search, Idea 1 will perform better.
  • Sector and service tags on each card double as links into the relevant sector pages — a useful cross-sell the current directory does not have.
Idea 3

Task-led — “find the right expert”

A different premise. Most visitors do not want to browse 423 people; they want the right person for a specific problem. This template leads with routes in — by question, by sector, by office — and keeps the full A–Z directory further down for those who want it.
